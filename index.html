Research in progress

Independent research · 2026–present

Energy measurement for edge inference

This study examines when operation counts cease to be reliable predictors of the energy consumed by neural network inference. A thorough qualitative analysis has been made on static predictors of the 43 trained and converted deployable TensorFlow Lite artifacts from a controlled model corpus. The next phase measures board-level inference energy and compares it with arithmetic and runtime indicators across different execution systems.

Research in progress

Independent research · 2026–present

Execution-verifiable safety evaluations for tool-using AI agents

This project studies the gap between what an AI agent reports and what it does through external tools. The proposed evaluation environment records attempted actions, executed effects and state changes independently of the agent allowing transcript-based judgments to be compared with verifiable execution evidence.

Current stage: research design and evaluator specification.

About

I have a BSc in Biomedical Engineering from the University of Lagos. My work is organised around the engineering design process: how can an observed need be translated into requirements, developed into a technical system and evaluated against the conditions in which that system will be used, particularly for biomedical applications?

The question first took shape during the 2020 UBORA–UNECA boot camp, where an automated hygiene system was developed with backup modes for interrupted power and water supply. It continued through the development of IncuBed and later AquaBot at Rice University. My present hardware research applies the same thinking to edge inference, testing whether convenient complexity measures reflect the energy a deployed system actually uses. In parallel, my software research examines whether claims made by tool-using AI agents agree with independently recorded execution evidence.
